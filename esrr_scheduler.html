How ESRR works here

  1. Work in rounds. A round ends when every ready process has had one turn.
  2. Within a round, always pick the ready process with the shortest remaining time (the SRTF rule). Ties go to the earlier arrival.
  3. Run it for at most one time quantum, then it waits for the next round (the Round Robin rule). If it needs less than a quantum, it finishes.
  4. A process that arrives mid-round can be picked straight away, so short newcomers do not wait for a full round.
  5. In dynamic mode the quantum for each round is the mean remaining time of the ready processes, rounded up.

The paper’s abstract says ESRR combines RR and SRTF, lowers total waiting and turnaround time compared with RR, and lets long processes finish faster than under SRTF. The full pseudocode was not available to us, so the steps above are our interpretation of that idea. Replace them with the paper’s exact steps if your guide’s copy differs.

Reference: P. Sinha, B. Prabadevi, S. Dutta, N. Deepa, N. Kumari, “Efficient Process Scheduling Algorithm using RR and SRTF,” ic-ETITE 2020, doi 10.1109/ic-ETITE47903.2020.101. Self Learning Activity, Operating System, SAKEC.
